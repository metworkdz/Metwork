Metwork Startup Academy

A premium video-course platform at metwork.dz/academy, on the same Metwork account. People buy a course for life or subscribe to the Academy Pass for everything. Mentors earn a share of every sale and of the subscription time spent on their courses, paid into the wallet they already use in the consultant portal. Video is DRM-protected and watermarked per viewer.

What you asked for

This is how I read the brief. Everything in the rest of the plan follows from these points, so correct me here first if something is off.

For learners

  • A catalogue of already-filmed courses, organised by field, Udemy/Coursera style: course, modules, lessons, cover video, thumbnail, ratings, completion certificate.
  • Two ways to get access: buy one course (lifetime, tied to the account) or the monthly subscription that opens every course while it is active.
  • Same Metwork login for entrepreneurs and freelancers. Premium members (Founder, Freelancer Premium) get 20% off courses and the subscription, shown in their perks.

For mentors and the admin

  • Admin creates courses and attaches a partner mentor from the existing mentors list. Any mentor can also build a course alone.
  • Each mentor gets a public profile listing their courses.
  • Mentors earn a percentage the admin sets per mentor: on each course sale, and on subscriptions in proportion to watch time. Earnings land in the mentor wallet in the consultant dashboard.
  • Nobody watches or downloads without paying.

Module 1 already exists (Trouver et valider son idée startup, 7 episodes, about 14 minutes, edited with the _studio pipeline). I used its real episode list in the player mockup.

Business model

All prices are in dinars, paid with the wallet or by CIB / Edahabia through the existing SlickPay checkout. The Pass price is a platform setting the admin can change at any time; a course price is set by its author when posting it, never below 2 900 DA.

Mentor revenue

The admin sets one percentage per mentor (default 30% mentor / 70% Metwork). It is copied onto each sale and each monthly statement when they are created, so a later change never rewrites past money. Credits go through the one existing function, applyMentorEarningToDraft in src/server/mentors/ledger.ts. No second credit path gets written.

Course sales

Mentor earning = amount the learner actually paid × the mentor’s share.

Course 2 900 DA, Premium buyer pays 2 320 DA
Mentor share 30% → 696 DA to the mentor

It is credited as pending and becomes withdrawable after the refund window (proposal: 7 days). A refund voids the pending credit with the existing voidPendingEarning.

Academy Pass, by watch time

I recommend splitting each subscriber’s own payment across the mentors that subscriber watched that month, rather than one global pool.

A global pool lets a mentor drain everyone’s money by inflating views. With a per-subscriber split, fake viewing can at most redistribute the cheater’s own 3 500 DA, so the fraud does not pay.

earning(mentor, month) = Σ over subscribers ( net paid × mentor share × minutes on mentor’s courses ÷ subscriber’s total minutes )

  • What counts as watching: the player sends a heartbeat every 30 s while the video is actually playing. The server counts wall-clock seconds, at most 35 s per heartbeat, one active stream per account, and caps any lesson at 2× its length per day, so leaving a video looping does not earn anything.
  • Settlement: a cron job on the 1st of each month closes the previous month, writes one statement line per subscriber × mentor, and credits the mentors. A subscriber who watched nothing leaves the whole payment with Metwork.
  • Mentor visibility: the Studio shows minutes watched and a running estimate during the month. The figure is fixed at settlement.

Content security

Several layers, each covering what the one before cannot.

1 · Encryption

Every video is DRM-encrypted (Widevine on Chrome/Android, FairPlay on Safari/iPhone). Download extensions and saving the file get unplayable fragments.

2 · Server-side gate

The browser never receives a video ID it can reuse. POST /api/academy/playback checks the one entitlement function, then asks VdoCipher for a playback token valid a few minutes, for that user and lesson only.

3 · Watermark

Name, masked phone and account code drift across the picture during playback. A leaked screen recording names the account that made it.

4 · Account sharing

One stream at a time per account, at most 3 registered devices, and the existing email-code login. Starting a second stream stops the first.

5 · Scraping alarms

Rate limit on token requests (Upstash, already in place). An account opening dozens of lessons per hour is paused for admin review and the admin is notified.

6 · Everything else private

Worksheets and PDFs use private Cloudinary assets with short signed links, as the pitch-deck and contract flows already do. The player only loads on metwork.dz (domain lock).

Known limits. No platform can stop someone filming a screen with a phone. On desktop Chrome, Widevine runs in software (L3), so screen-capture tools can still record; Safari, iPhone and most Android phones block it. In those cases the watermark identifies the account, and the terms of use plus a takedown process do the rest. Promising more than that would be false.

Languages and theme

Translated course content

Course titles, subtitles, outcomes, module titles and lesson titles are stored in French, English and Arabic, with the language they were written in. A visitor sees their language; if a translation is missing they see the original. When an author saves, the missing languages are drafted automatically (Haiku 4.5, within the AI cost rule) and the author or admin can correct them. The videos themselves stay in their recorded language, shown as a label (e.g. “Darija + français”).

Light and dark

The Academy follows the phone or computer setting by default, and a sun / auto / moon switch in its bar changes it at any time (remembered on that device). The rest of Metwork stays light as today. The light home is the first mockup; the other learner mockups show the dark palette.
